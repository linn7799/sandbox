# Film recommendations – November 2026

Based on my Letterboxd export (558 films; ratings up to 2020) and my stated preferences. None of these are logged on my Letterboxd. Availability checked on JustWatch Sweden on 2026-11-04. It changes often, so check again before watching.

**Taste signals:** 5★ for *Burning*, *Winter Sleep*, *The Square*, *Three Colours: Red* and *Sorry We Missed You*; 2★ for *Marriage Story*. Lots of Kore-eda, Iranian, Indian independent and Nordic film. Cineasterna is used heavily.

**Top three if short on time:** Monster, Hit the Road, Smoke Sauna Sisterhood.

## Cineasterna (free with a library card)

1. **Monster** (Hirokazu Kore-eda, Japan 2023, Cannes Best Screenplay). I've seen five Kore-eda films but not this one. It tells one story three times from different viewpoints, so it plays like a mystery, and it ends with a very tender final act. Ryuichi Sakamoto's last film score.
2. **Drive My Car** (Ryusuke Hamaguchi, Japan 2021, Cannes Best Screenplay). I've seen Hamaguchi's *Evil Does Not Exist*. This one is three hours long, very calm and carried by its dialogue. Suits anyone who liked the slow pace of *Winter Sleep*. Also on Draken Film.
3. **Hit the Road** (Panah Panahi, Iran 2021, Cannes Directors' Fortnight). A family road trip that's funny for most of its length and sad underneath, with a little boy who never stops talking. Mostly long shots of landscape. Fits with the Kiarostami films I've seen, but warmer and stranger.
4. **Close** (Lukas Dhont, Belgium 2022, Cannes Grand Prix). About two 13-year-old best friends who drift apart. Quiet and sad, with striking colours (flower fields, ice rinks).
5. **Aftersun** (Charlotte Wells, UK 2022, Cannes Critics' Week). A daughter remembers a holiday with her father, pieced together from camcorder footage. Built from small details; the last ten minutes are among the most moving of the decade.
6. **Flow** (Gints Zilbalodis, Latvia 2024). Animated, no dialogue, about a cat in a flooded world. Everything is told through sound and the animation. Very unusual. It won the Oscar, but it's an outsider film.
7. **Robot Dreams** (Pablo Berger, Spain 2023, premiered at Cannes). Animated, no dialogue: a dog and a robot in 1980s New York. Looks like a light comedy but is surprisingly heartbreaking. For when I want warmth.
8. **In the Mood for Love** (Wong Kar-wai, Hong Kong 2000, Cannes Best Actor and technical prize). On the list for the cinematography and sound alone: Christopher Doyle's camerawork and the waltz theme. Cineasterna's version may have only Chinese subtitles, so check before starting. Also on MUBI.

## SVT Play (free)

9. **Petite maman** (Céline Sciamma, France 2021). From the director of *Portrait of a Lady on Fire*. A 72-minute fairy tale about an eight-year-old who meets her mother as a child. Simple, strange and gentle. JustWatch already lists it on SVT Play, and the press says SVT also gets it on 20 Nov. Also on Cineasterna.
10. **Smoke Sauna Sisterhood / Bastusystrar** (Anna Hints, Estonia 2023, Sundance directing award). Women in a dark smoke sauna talk about their bodies, shame and grief. A documentary that feels like a ritual: steam, firelight and very physical sound design. Also on Cineasterna.
11. **Sick of Myself** (Kristoffer Borgli, Norway 2022, Cannes Un Certain Regard). A very black comedy about a woman who makes herself ill on purpose to get attention. The most uncomfortable and quirky pick here, in the same vein as *The Square* and *Triangle of Sadness*. Two press listings say it arrives on SVT Play on 6 Nov, but this wasn't confirmed on SVT Play itself.

## Netflix

12. **Pedro Páramo** (Rodrigo Prieto, Mexico 2024). Directed by the cinematographer of *Brokeback Mountain* and *Killers of the Flower Moon*, adapting Juan Rulfo's novel about a ghost town. Looks dreamlike and the timeline jumps around. Mixed reviews, but strong on craft.
13. **His House** (Remi Weekes, UK 2020). A Sudanese refugee couple are placed in a haunted council house in England. A horror film that's really about trauma and guilt; smarter and more inventive than most genre films.

## Also worth it

- **Roma** (Alfonso Cuarón, Mexico 2018, Venice Golden Lion). On Netflix, worth it even if I skipped it for being "the Oscar film".
- **First Cow** (Kelly Reichardt, USA 2019). On Cineasterna now; comes to SVT Play on 20 Nov.

## Not free anywhere right now (rent or other services only)

- *Anatomy of a Fall* and *Godland*: rent only.
- *Sentimental Value*: Prime Video only.
- Watchlist titles *Aquarius* and *I Am Not a Witch* weren't checked.
